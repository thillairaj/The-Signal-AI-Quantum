Six things a leader should hold on to

Assume breach

Prevention will sometimes fail. Organisations that plan for detection, containment and recovery suffer incidents; organisations that plan only for prevention suffer catastrophes.

Identity is the perimeter

Most attacks begin with a person or a password. MFA everywhere, least privilege and disciplined joiner-mover-leaver processes close more real risk than most new tools.

Basics beat novelty

Patching, segmentation, EDR coverage and tested backups defeat the attacks that actually happen. Fund the unglamorous fundamentals before the fashionable platform.

Speed is the metric

MTTD and MTTR, honestly measured, tell you whether you are improving. Attackers measure their dwell time too; the shorter yours, the smaller the incident.

Your vendors are your surface

Supply chain and SaaS compromises arrive through trust you granted. Evidence-based vendor diligence is part of your security posture, not procurement paperwork.

Rehearsal is the control

Tabletop exercises, restore tests and incident decision rights agreed in calm weather are what you will actually use in the storm. Nothing else survives contact with a real crisis.

A leader's security checklist

  • Is MFA enforced on email, VPN, cloud consoles and finance systems, with an exceptions list close to zero?
  • What are our patch ages for critical, internet-facing vulnerabilities this month?
  • What percentage of endpoints and servers actually run EDR, and which are the gaps?
  • If one office PC were compromised tonight, what could it reach by morning?
  • When was the last timed restore test of our backups, and what failed?
  • Are MTTD and MTTR measured honestly, by severity, and trending the right way?
  • Who can take systems offline, notify regulators and speak publicly in an incident — decided in writing?
  • Do our critical vendors provide current security evidence, not just assurances?
  • When did the executive team last run a tabletop exercise, and were its findings closed?
  • Is our top-risk register reviewed quarterly, with named owners and recorded risk acceptance?

An honest note

No organisation is unbreachable, and no vendor product changes that. State-backed groups, criminal affiliates and simple mistakes all get through sometimes, including at well-run companies with strong teams.

The leadership task is not to promise perfect security. It is to make compromise survivable: detected fast, contained small, recovered cleanly, and communicated honestly.

Educational only, for leadership literacy. It is not a substitute for a professional security assessment of your own environment, systems and obligations.
