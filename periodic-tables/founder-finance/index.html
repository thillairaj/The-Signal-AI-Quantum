Key founder formulas

Gross Profit

Revenue − COGS

Gross Margin

(Gross Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100

Runway

Available Cash ÷ Monthly Net Cash Burn (simplified estimate)

LTV : CAC

Customer Lifetime Value ÷ Customer Acquisition Cost

Post-money Valuation

Pre-money Valuation + New Investment

Investor Ownership

New Investment ÷ Post-money Valuation (simplified primary-equity example)

A simple fundraising example

Worked example from the guide

₹20 crore pre-money + ₹5 crore raised = ₹25 crore post-money

The new investor receives 20% of the post-money company. Existing shareholders collectively retain about 80% — before option-pool changes, convertible instruments, or special terms.

Real documents are more complex: liquidation preferences, anti-dilution provisions and multiple share classes can materially change the economics.

The founder journey

Start the company → Generate revenue → Understand unit economics → Manage cash → Raise capital → Understand valuation → Manage dilution and ownership → Scale → Exit or create liquidity.

Crowdfunding reminder

Reward, donation, equity and debt crowdfunding have different legal availability, investor protections, disclosure rules and tax treatment by jurisdiction. Verify local regulations before launching a campaign.

Founder checklist — before accepting capital

  • Why do we need the money, and how much do we actually need?
  • What milestone will this capital help us reach?
  • How many months of runway will it provide?
  • What valuation is being proposed, and how much ownership will be diluted?
  • What happens to the ESOP pool?
  • Are there liquidation preferences, anti-dilution or pro-rata rights?
  • What board or information rights are being granted?
  • Is the capital debt, equity, SAFE, convertible note, grant, or another instrument?
  • What are the repayment obligations, if any?
  • What happens in a future funding round?
  • What happens if the company is sold?
