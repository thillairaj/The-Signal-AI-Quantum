How to read this landscape

Frameworks vs platforms

Code frameworks (LangGraph, CrewAI, the vendor SDKs) give engineering teams control and portability. Enterprise platforms (Copilot Studio, Agentforce, ServiceNow) give governance and speed inside one ecosystem. Most large organisations end up with both: platforms for business-led agents, frameworks for product-critical ones.

Open-source gravity

Most of the builder layer is open-source or source-available: LangChain, AutoGen, Browser Use, Langfuse, Promptfoo. That lowers entry cost and raises your responsibility: you own upgrades, security patching and support. Price that ownership into comparisons with SaaS platforms.

The MCP effect

The Model Context Protocol standardised how agents connect to tools and data, so tool support is becoming a checklist item rather than a differentiator. When comparing frameworks and platforms, ask how each implements MCP for tools you already own, instead of counting raw integrations.

Eval is the gap

Almost every organisation can build an agent demo; few can prove the agent still works after a model or prompt change. The evaluation and observability category (LangSmith, Langfuse, Promptfoo and their peers) is where professional agent programmes are quietly decided.

Consolidation is coming

This table has over fifty tiles for a market barely three years old. Acquisitions, renames and shutdowns are certain: editors, frameworks and voice platforms are already changing owners and names. Prefer tools with clear ownership, export paths and standards support over feature-count winners.

Models vs tools

The tool is not the intelligence: most tiles here orchestrate models made by someone else. A tool's value is its workflow, integrations and controls. Judge the pairing separately: pick the tool category first, then check which models it lets you use and swap.

A leader's tool-selection checklist

  • Which category is this, and is that the category our problem actually belongs to?
  • Build or buy: do we need framework control, or platform governance and speed?
  • What data and systems will the agent touch, and who approved those permissions?
  • How will we evaluate the agent before launch, and after every model or prompt change?
  • What is the exit path: can we export prompts, data, traces and workflows if we switch?
  • What does it cost at production volume, not at pilot volume?
  • Has security reviewed the tool's data handling, residency and retention?
  • Is the tool model-independent, or are we accepting single-vendor coupling on purpose?
  • Who owns the tool: a named internal owner for upgrades, incidents and vendor contact?
  • Does it expose traces and logs our observability stack can actually consume?
  • Where are the human approval points for actions that spend money or contact customers?
  • Is the pilot scoped to 4 to 6 weeks with success measures agreed in advance?

An honest note on this market

Positions in this table are stated as of October 2026, and some will already be stale within a quarter: products in this space are renamed, acquired or folded into larger platforms at unusual speed.

Use the table to learn the categories and build a shortlist of two or three tools per job, then verify the current state of each shortlisted tool directly with the vendor before any commitment. Category knowledge ages in years; brand facts age in months.
