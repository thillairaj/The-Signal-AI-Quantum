Six things a leader should hold on to about Google Cloud

Data gravity decides

BigQuery, Spanner and Looker are the gravity wells of this cloud. Organisations rarely choose Google Cloud for generic hosting; they choose it because one analytical or global-data workload is decisively better here, and everything else follows that workload.

Kubernetes is home turf

Google created Kubernetes, and GKE reflects a decade of internal cluster experience. If your strategy is container-based and portability matters, Google Cloud starts the evaluation with a genuine home advantage.

AI is woven in, not bolted on

Gemini, the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform and TPUs form one continuous stack from prototype to governed production. The advantage is integration: your data platform, security model and AI platform are the same platform, with the same controls.

Judge it workload by workload

Google Cloud is the third-largest hyperscaler and does not need to win everything. Score each significant workload on data, containers, AI, cost mechanics and local support, and let the strongest two or three workloads anchor the relationship.

Pricing rewards commitment and steadiness

Sustained use discounts arrive automatically, and committed use discounts reward honest forecasting with savings of more than half on eligible compute. The flip side: undisciplined BigQuery querying and over-commitment erase the advantage quickly, so FinOps discipline is part of the architecture.

Sovereignty is a product line

Assured Workloads, organisation policies, external key management and partner-operated sovereign offerings mean regulated workloads have a designed path here. Verify your exact regime early, because the details differ by country and sector.

A leader's Google Cloud checklist

Before you commit

  • Pick two or three anchor workloads where Google is demonstrably strongest, usually data, Kubernetes or AI
  • Design the organisation, folder and project hierarchy, and the VPC foundation, before the first workload lands
  • Turn on the detailed billing export to BigQuery and project-level budget alerts from day one
  • Confirm support tiers and partner coverage in the countries you actually operate in
  • Check regulated workloads against Assured Workloads and sovereign offerings before architects assume coverage

In the first year

  • Set organisation policies for regions, public access and service account keys as enforced guardrails
  • Standardise new builds on Cloud Run or GKE Autopilot unless a team can argue otherwise
  • Negotiate committed use discounts against measured baselines, committing to the floor of usage, not the peak
  • Put BigQuery cost guardrails in place: quotas, slot commitments and per-team cost visibility
  • Audit IAM from the top of the hierarchy down at least quarterly, and review the dormant-access report

An honest note

Google Cloud is typically the third estate in most enterprises: its partner network, local talent pool and enterprise hand-holding are thinner than the two larger rivals in many markets, and its catalogue, while deep in data and AI, is narrower elsewhere. None of that cancels its real strengths in analytics, containers and AI, but it does mean the workload-by-workload discipline in this table is not a slogan.
