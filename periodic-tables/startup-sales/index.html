B2B Sales for Founders

The vocabulary of selling to businesses, in plain language — for founders who are their company's first salesperson.

Core principle: Early sales is learning, not theatre. The founder sells first to discover what the market will actually pay for, who feels the pain most, and why deals really close. Only once that pattern is clear should you build the machine — hires, playbooks, tooling — that repeats it without you.

Six things a founder should hold on to

Sell before you hire

The founder's first job is not managing a sales team; it is discovering, personally, who buys, why they buy and what the deal requires. A sales hire amplifies a pattern that exists — it cannot invent one.

Qualification is kindness

Every unqualified deal in your pipeline steals time from a qualified one and from your product. Frameworks like BANT and MEDDPICC exist to make 'no' arrive early, when it is cheap.

Activity is not progress

Meetings held and proposals sent feel like momentum. Progress is only what the customer has done: stakeholders met, criteria agreed, paper process started. Manage to their movement, not your motion.

Price is a value conversation

Discounts offered before the cost of the problem is established are pure donation. Anchor on the cost of inaction, trade concessions instead of giving them, and protect the price that funds your roadmap.

Deals die in the paper, not the pitch

Security reviews, legal redlines and procurement steps kill more late-stage deals than competitors do. Map the paper process early, with your champion, and work backwards from the signing date.

Write the playbook from wins

Your first ten wins contain your entire sales system in embryo — the ICP that actually bought, the questions that surfaced pain, the path each deal took. Document that truthfully and hiring becomes replication, not experimentation.

A founder's sales checklist

Pipeline & qualification

  • Can I describe my ICP as a list of named companies, not adjectives?
  • Does every opportunity have a named next step the customer agreed to?
  • Is my pipeline coverage at least 3x this period's target?
  • For each late-stage deal, have I met the economic buyer?
  • Do I know each big deal's decision process and paper process?

Deal discipline

  • Is the pain quantified in the customer's own numbers?
  • Do I have a tested champion, or only a friendly contact?
  • Am I multi-threaded — at least three relationships in the account?
  • Does every POC have written success criteria and an end date?
  • Have I traded every concession instead of giving it away?

Worked example: pipeline coverage

Your target for next quarter is ₹40 lakh in new bookings. Your honest win rate on qualified opportunities is 25 percent — one in four closes. Required pipeline = 40 lakh / 0.25 = ₹1.6 crore of qualified pipeline by the quarter's start, a coverage ratio of 4x. If today your qualified pipeline is ₹90 lakh (2.25x coverage), the maths — not optimism — says the quarter will likely land near ₹22 lakh unless you add roughly ₹70 lakh of qualified pipeline now. Coverage is a prospecting alarm that rings one quarter early.
