The standards, laws and certifications that shape what good security looks like — and what leaders are accountable for.

Core principle: Frameworks are floors, not ceilings. Compliance proves a baseline was met on audit day; security is what happens every other day. Use frameworks to set the minimum and to structure the conversation with the board, then invest past the floor where your real risks live.

Six things a leader should hold on to

Compliance is a floor, not a ceiling

Every framework in this table describes a minimum. Meeting it means you are defensible; it does not mean you are secure. Your real risk profile decides how far past the floor you invest.

Pick a spine, then map

Choose one primary framework — NIST CSF, ISO 27001 or CIS Controls — as your organising spine, then map everything else to it. Obligations multiply; your control set should not.

Reports are not certificates

SOC 2 is an auditor's report over a period; ISO 27001 is a certificate on a cycle. They answer different questions. Read SOC 2 exceptions, check ISO scope, and never accept either at cover value.

Regulators now name the board

NIS2 makes management bodies accountable, the SEC demands board oversight disclosure, and Indian regulators require board-approved policies. Governance is no longer delegable to the CISO alone.

Clocks are the real test

CERT-In gives you 6 hours, the RBI 2 to 6, NIS2 a 24-hour early warning, the SEC four business days after materiality. Incident processes that cannot meet the fastest clock you face are not finished.

Your vendors are your compliance surface

DORA, DPDP and every sector framework push obligations into your supply chain — and pull your vendors' failures onto your record. Tier them, evidence them, rehearse an exit.

A leader's compliance checklist

Know your obligations

  • List every framework, law and regulator that applies, by entity and country
  • Name one accountable owner per obligation
  • Choose a primary framework as the control spine
  • Map all other requirements to that spine
  • Track audit and attestation dates on one calendar

Operate, do not just attest

  • Review the exceptions inside every report you rely on, including your own
  • Check ISMS and report scopes before trusting any certificate
  • Test incident reporting against the fastest regulatory clock you face
  • Review third-party evidence at renewal, not after an incident
  • Report audit status and top real risks to the board together

The honest note

No organisation in this table's audience has ever been breached because it lacked a framework — breaches happen in the gap between the documented control and the operating one. Treat every certificate, including your own, as a claim to be spot-checked, and treat the audit as the floor of the conversation with your board, never the whole of it.

Educational only. Frameworks, laws and applicability change over time and differ by sector and country; confirm what applies to your organisation with qualified legal and compliance counsel.
