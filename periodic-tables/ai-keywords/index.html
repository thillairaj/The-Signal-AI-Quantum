Six things a leader should hold on to

Fluency is not competence

LLMs produce confident, polished language because that is what they are trained to produce. Correctness is a separate property that must be engineered with grounding, retrieval and verification, and tested on your own material.

Your data is the moat

Models are rented and increasingly similar. What competitors cannot copy is your proprietary data, your workflow integration and your customers' trust. AI strategy is mostly data and workflow strategy wearing a new name.

Demos lie by omission

Every demo runs on material chosen to succeed. The only evidence that matters is an evaluation on your documents, your customers and your edge cases, with failures counted, not explained away.

Cost lives at inference

Training makes headlines, but the bill that repeats forever is per-request inference: tokens in, tokens out, at your real volumes. Model the unit economics of a completed task, not a single chat.

Agents multiply errors

Each autonomous step is a chance to go wrong, and errors compound across steps. Autonomy should be granted per workflow, where steps are verifiable and mistakes are recoverable, never by product label.

Governance enables speed

Clear rules, a model inventory and a fast approval path let good teams move quickly without gambling the company. Governance that arrives after deployment is archaeology, not management.

A leader's AI checklist

  • Name the business outcome and baseline metric before any pilot starts
  • Evaluate on your own data and edge cases, with failures counted and reviewed
  • Choose RAG for facts that change, fine-tuning for format and behaviour
  • Contract clearly on data: training use, retention, region and exit rights
  • Set latency and cost budgets per completed task, not per demo
  • Keep a human accountable for every consequential AI output
  • Red-team systems before launch and after major changes
  • Maintain an inventory of models and AI uses, with owners
  • Map your uses against the EU AI Act risk tiers and your local rules
  • Give staff an approved tool and a clear data rule, in that order
  • Review ROI against the baseline on a fixed date, then scale or stop

An honest note on hype

AI is genuinely transformative and persistently oversold, at the same time. Capabilities improve fast, demos outrun reliability, and timelines from every direction, boosters and critics alike, have been wrong in both directions.

The durable response is boring and effective: small funded experiments, evidence on your own ground, and scaling only what survives measurement. Leaders who run that loop will not need to predict the future of AI; they will profit from whichever version arrives.

Educational only, for leadership literacy. AI capabilities and product claims change quickly, so verify current vendor statements before making investment or procurement decisions.
