Six things a leader should hold on to

We are in the NISQ era

Today's machines are small and noisy research instruments. Useful for learning and pilots, not for production workloads. Anyone selling transformation today is ahead of the physics.

Advantage is problem-specific

Quantum helps only where a known algorithm fits the problem's shape: simulation, factoring, some search and optimisation. Most business problems have no quantum shortcut.

The crypto risk is present-day

Harvest now, decrypt later makes long-lived data vulnerable today. NIST finalised post-quantum standards in August 2024. Migration planning is the one quantum action that cannot wait for the technology.

Qubit counts mislead

Ask for error rates, and how many logical (error-corrected) qubits a roadmap delivers. A smaller, cleaner machine beats a larger, noisier one on real circuits.

Hybrid is the pattern

Near-term quantum runs inside classical workflows: QPU as accelerator, classical systems in control. Your data, pipelines and people stay central.

Sensing is nearer than computing

Quantum sensing, navigation and QRNG are closer to deployment and revenue than universal quantum computing, and almost never make the headlines.

A leader's quantum checklist

  • Inventory your cryptography: where RSA and ECC protect data, and how long that data must stay secret
  • Start PQC migration planning for long-lived data, following the NIST standards
  • Identify one or two candidate use cases worth a small, benchmarked pilot, simulation first
  • Build literacy in your leadership and architecture teams (this table is a start)
  • Track logical-qubit and error-rate milestones, not press-release qubit counts
  • Access hardware through cloud QaaS for experiments; do not buy machines
  • Budget quantum as R&D learning and risk management, not transformation

An honest note on timelines

Expert estimates for a cryptographically relevant, fault-tolerant quantum computer range from years to decades, and the field's best roadmaps have slipped before.

The leadership response is not to predict the date. It is to remove the risk that does not depend on the date (cryptography), and to buy cheap optionality (literacy, pilots, partnerships) on the upside.
