Six things a leader should hold on to

1. Counts are marketing; errors are reality

A qubit count tells you almost nothing by itself. Error rates, fidelities and logical-qubit progress tell you whether a machine is a milestone or a prop.

2. No modality has won

Superconducting, ion, atom, photon and spin platforms each lead on different dimensions. Anyone certain about the winner in 2026 is selling something.

3. The machine is mostly not the chip

Refrigeration, wiring, control electronics and calibration dominate cost, size and scaling limits. Hardware due diligence that stops at the processor is incomplete.

4. Below-threshold is the line that matters

Error correction only works when physical error rates fall under a threshold. Willow crossed it on real hardware in 2024; every roadmap is implicitly about that crossing.

5. Benchmarks are arguments, not verdicts

Supremacy claims, Quantum Volume and CLOPS each measure something real and omit something real. Read what a benchmark leaves out before repeating what it claims.

6. Fabrication is the moat

Useful machines need millions of nearly identical qubits. The winners will be decided as much in fabs as in physics papers.

A leader's hardware checklist

Questions for any hardware vendor

  • Are your quoted qubits physical or logical, and what is the logical roadmap?
  • What are your two-qubit gate fidelity and readout fidelity — typical, not best-day?
  • Is your physical error rate below your code's threshold, with data?
  • How do wiring, control electronics and heat budget scale to your next machine?
  • What is your fabrication yield, and on what wafer size?

Reading a hardware announcement

  • Separate the peer-reviewed result from the press release around it.
  • Check whether classical simulations have since narrowed the claim.
  • Look for logical-qubit and error-rate numbers before qubit counts.
  • Note who else has replicated or independently benchmarked the result.
  • Ask what a customer can actually run on the system today.

An honest note on timelines

No vendor can currently sell you a fault-tolerant quantum computer, and honest roadmaps put broadly useful machines years away, behind error-correction milestones that are only beginning to be demonstrated. Buy hardware access today for learning, benchmarking and team readiness — priced as research — and treat any promise of near-term quantum advantage for your specific workload as a claim to be tested, never a plan to be funded.
