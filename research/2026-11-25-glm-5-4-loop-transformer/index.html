Why Does Z.ai's GLM-5.4 Run the Same Layers Twice?

A 750B model trades parameters for depth, but its headline claim still has no benchmark behind it.

25 Nov 2026 · By The Signal

On November 23, 2026, Z.ai released GLM-5.4, its first flagship built on a Loop Transformer. It runs 28 of the GLM-5 base model's layers twice per token, turning 78 stored layers into 106 layer passes. Z.ai says the roughly 750-billion-parameter model matches models with 2 to 3 trillion parameters. The claim is bold. The evidence is not the flagship itself.

Depth without new weights

A standard transformer runs each layer once per forward pass. A loop transformer sends tokens through a shared block of layers a second time. The idea dates to the 2018 Universal Transformer. Z.ai did not train one from scratch because looped models trained from zero still lag unshared models at the same pretraining budget. So it converted the already trained GLM-5 base through continued training, with three fixes for the 750-billion scale: per-token gates controlling how much each pass writes back, a hybrid KV cache that adds only a sliding window for the second pass, and reuse of the first pass's sparse attention indices. It validated the design at 1, 4, 30 and 750 billion parameters.

What the numbers actually show

The strongest evidence Z.ai published comes from a 30-billion-parameter model, not the flagship. At the same size and about 10 percent more training compute, the looped version improved AIME25 from 19.2 to 30.4 and GPQA-Diamond from 47.6 to 56.3, while cutting output length by 10 to 14 percent on most benchmarks. In effect, part of the reasoning moves from written tokens into the loop's hidden depth. Z.ai also reports up to 200 tokens per second off peak and 70 to 80 at peak. No flagship benchmark scores have been published, and the weights are promised only after safety hardening, with no date. It is available now through the GLM Coding Plan, ZCode and Z.ai Chat. Z.ai admits one flaw: the model can fall into marathon thinking. The same week, Thinking Machines shipped Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weights model that loops its middle layers three times per token. The test that matters is independent: benchmark the flagship, or the 2-to-3-trillion claim stays a vendor number.
