How Does Multi-Head Latent Attention Shrink the KV Cache?

DeepSeek's MLA cuts the KV cache 93 percent, and eight model families followed

6 Nov 2026 · By The Signal

Signal Research Notes is an original-research series on The Signal, published three times a week. Each edition asks one question, works through it from primary sources, and keeps confirmed facts separate from analysis. Sources are linked at the end.

Every token a model generates forces it to remember every token before it. That memory is the KV cache: the key and value vectors for each token, layer and head. The cache makes generation fast and long context expensive, because it grows with every token and every user.

Compress by rank, not by head count

Standard multi-head attention stores a full key and value for every head: 128 heads of 128 dimensions in BF16 cost 65,536 bytes per token per layer. Multi-Head Latent Attention, introduced in DeepSeek-V2 (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv:2405.04434, May 2024), takes a different axis. It down-projects each token into one shared low-rank latent vector, 512 dimensions in DeepSeek-V3, caching only that. Up-projection matrices rebuild the per-head keys and values at attention time, absorbed into other weights so the full vectors never materialise. The paper reports a 93.3 percent cut in KV cache size against standard multi-head attention at matching quality. DeepSeek-V3 needs 70KB of KV cache per token, against 516KB for LLaMA-3.1 405B and 327KB for Qwen2.5-72B (arXiv:2412.19437, December 2024).

The position problem, and the fix

One wrinkle is position: rotary embeddings rotate each key by its position, and that rotation does not survive MLA's matrix absorption. DeepSeek-V2 solves it with decoupled RoPE: a separate 64-dimension key carries position outside the compressed latent. The cost becomes 2 times (512 plus 64) times 2 bytes, or 1,152 bytes per token per layer, a 57 times reduction regardless of head count.

Why it matters, and what it costs

A smaller cache buys longer context or more concurrent users on the same GPU, which is why at least eight model families beyond DeepSeek adopted MLA. The catch is commitment: latent, projections and decoupled RoPE split are trained in from the start. A grouped-query team cannot flip a flag for MLA's savings; it needs retraining. Compression is a design decision, not a serving setting.
