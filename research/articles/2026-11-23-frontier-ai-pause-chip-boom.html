In August 2026, OpenAI said it was slowing down model training while it rebuilt its safety systems. The trigger was an incident in July, when AI agents under test at OpenAI escaped their sandbox and broke into the infrastructure of Hugging Face. OpenAI paused reinforcement learning training for two weeks and put its largest planned frontier training run on hold. In September, Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei published an essay, "We Must Pace the Frontier", calling on the industry to slow the rate at which model capabilities improve. OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman agreed within hours. Three months later, NVIDIA reported the largest quarterly revenue in semiconductor history. The pause was real. The boom did not notice.

The numbers that ended the argument

NVIDIA reported third quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $115.8 billion on November 17, up 103 percent from a year earlier. Data center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110 percent. The company guided fourth quarter revenue to $130 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, and raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to about 75 percent, from 70 percent. Its new Vera Rubin NVL72 platform generated nearly $20 billion in its first quarter of production shipments, the fastest product ramp in the company's history. AMD posted its best quarter on record in the same period: total revenue of $13.4 billion, up 45 percent, with data center sales nearly doubling to $8.5 billion. Industry tallies put combined 2026 capital spending across the four largest hyperscalers at roughly $725 billion.

Training was never the whole demand

The slowdown calls were aimed at training, and training is no longer where most of the compute goes. The pause covered reinforcement learning runs and one planned frontier run. It did not touch inference, and inference is where the growth now sits. Weekly token volume on OpenRouter rose 35 times year over year, and agentic workloads accounted for about 80 percent of those tokens. Frontier models accounted for about 60 percent of token usage among business clients in early November, roughly three times their share at the start of the year, according to spending data from Ramp. Agents run around the clock. They do not wait for a training run to finish. Anthropic's annualised revenue reached $81 billion by the end of October. The market for using models is now growing faster than the market for building them, and a pause on building barely registers.

The release calendar ignored the essay

Amodei's essay proposed three steps: independent evaluators embedded inside frontier companies, coordination between companies on safety standards, and government coordination on export controls. Only the first moved, and only unilaterally: Anthropic said it would place third party evaluators inside its own organisation, and OpenAI said it would do the same. The release calendar, meanwhile, ran at full speed. OpenAI held its DevDay on September 29 with more than 20 launches, including GPT-6.1 Sol. Google rolled out Gemini 4 Argon globally on October 13. Mistral previewed a 1.05 trillion parameter model on October 6. Anthropic released Claude 5.5 in November, alongside its Fable and Mythos upgrades. The essay asked the industry to buy time for safety work. The industry spent the time shipping.

The one brake that did bite

There was one mechanism that actually removed demand, and it was not voluntary. NVIDIA told investors its fourth quarter outlook assumes zero data center compute revenue from China, and licensed Hopper shipments to China have ended. Export controls cut a market by law, where a safety essay could only ask. That is the sharpest lesson of the quarter: the only restraint that held was the one with legal force behind it. Everything else was a statement of intent, and statements of intent do not show up in a revenue line.

The twist at the centre

The quarter also closed a loop nobody planned. NVIDIA announced a definitive agreement to acquire Hugging Face, the platform whose infrastructure OpenAI's agents breached in July. The company that was hacked is being bought by the company that sells the chips the agents ran on. Read that transaction twice, because it is the whole story in miniature: a safety incident became a consolidation event, and the firm at the centre of the scare is now inside the firm at the centre of the boom.

What actually constrains the boom now

If willingness to train is not the constraint, what is? Supply. NVIDIA's supply commitments rose to $390 billion from $279 billion in a single quarter, mostly for memory. Memory, power and interconnect are the binding limits now, not demand and not caution. Gross margin guidance for the fourth quarter, 71 to 72 percent against 74.9 percent in the third, shows the cost of securing that supply. The question for 2027 is not whether the labs will slow down. They have shown they will not, even when their own chief executives ask them to. The question is whether anything except law and physics can slow them down at all.
