On October 27, 2026, Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 as a research preview for trusted testers, with wider access to follow in Project Genie and the Gemini API and AI Studio. The headline change from Genie 3 is sound: Genie 4 is DeepMind's first real time world model to generate audio natively, with music, ambient noise, and dialogue produced alongside the world.

What is new

A world model turns a prompt or an image into an explorable scene. Genie 4 adds three things. It can be steered live: a typed prompt can change the weather, move the camera, or add a person or an object while the simulation runs, and Gemini adapts the prompt to fit the scene. Characters move naturally and can converse and react in real time. Reasoning sits inside the loop: Gemini Omni anchors how objects look and behave, while Gemini 3.8 Flash observes the scene, breaks a goal into steps, and checks each frame against the plan.

Why it matters

The shift is from generating a clip to simulating a system. A clip is judged on how it looks; a world is judged on whether events play out coherently when the user intervenes. DeepMind positions Genie 4 as training ground material: robot and agent developers can test navigation before the real world, and game developers can prototype an idea before writing code.

The limits are stated plainly in the announcement. Visual details and object permanence can still drift after repeated edits, and complex physics, audio accuracy, legible text, and long term consistency remain research problems. All generated video and audio carries DeepMind's SynthID watermark, which matters as simulated scenes grow harder to distinguish from recorded ones. Genie 4 is best read as a research instrument, not a production pipeline: a way to study how agents behave in worlds that answer back.
