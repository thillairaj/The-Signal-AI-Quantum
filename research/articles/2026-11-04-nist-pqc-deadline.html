On October 27, 2026, NIST published the final version of Interagency Report 8547, Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards, replacing its November 12, 2024 draft. NIST states it will deprecate quantum-vulnerable public-key cryptography after 2030 and disallow its use under NIST standards and guidelines after 2035. The report is not a claim that such a computer exists today, only the timetable for retiring the algorithms it would break.

What changed from the draft

The main change is scope. The draft deprecated only the 112-bit tier after 2030, such as RSA-2048 and P-224. The final report removes that distinction: every approved quantum-vulnerable algorithm is deprecated after 2030, at every strength, and disallowed after 2035. Key establishment moves to ML-KEM under FIPS 203, with ML-KEM-768 as the recommended default; signatures move to ML-DSA under FIPS 204 and SLH-DSA under FIPS 205.

Why the federal dates arrive earlier

Under Executive Order 14412 and Office of Management and Budget Memorandum M-26-15, high-value assets and high-impact federal systems must adopt post-quantum key establishment by December 31, 2030, and post-quantum signatures by December 31, 2031, and support TLS 1.3 by January 2, 2030. The broader 2035 completion target remains. The report covers US federal systems, so its dates are not a worldwide mandate, but vendors following NIST guidance should expect them to flow down through procurement.

What to do now

The wrong reading is to treat 2035 as the year to begin. Deprecation after 2030 means new systems should not be built on algorithms already scheduled for retirement, and long-lived data comes first: anything encrypted today that must stay secret into the 2030s is exposed to harvest now, decrypt later collection. Start with inventory, migrate key establishment first where lifetimes are longest, and use hybrid ML-KEM with a classical scheme during the transition.
