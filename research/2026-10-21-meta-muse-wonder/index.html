Meta Ships Muse Wonder: Its First Frontier Model, Built for Agents That Remember

Active Memory and Active Focus move the frontier pitch from raw capability to persistent behavior

21 Oct 2026 · By The Signal

Signal Research Notes is an original-research series on The Signal, published three times a week. Each edition asks one question, works through it from primary sources, and keeps confirmed facts separate from analysis. Sources are linked at the end.

On October 12, Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, the first models the company calls frontier. The pitch is behavioral, not raw capability: Active Memory carries context across conversations, while Active Focus keeps an agent working through blockers and checking in before consequential actions. Meta frames the series as its foundation for personal and professional agents.

Muse Wonder ships through the Meta Model API and Muse Code at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. Wonder Full, the stronger variant, is exclusive to Muse Code subscribers and is not sold through the API. Both were trained at Meta's gigawatt-scale Prometheus data center, with a one-million-token context window and multimodal input.

What independent testing shows

Meta claims scores comparable to Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra, but published no benchmark numbers. Artificial Analysis scores Wonder at 52 and Wonder Full at 56 on its Intelligence Index v4.3, behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 58. The standout is agentic work: Wonder Full reaches 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, against 33% for Meta's Muse Spark 1.3, and 13.1% on Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark, passing only, against 8.6% for GPT-6 Astra.

Two caveats matter. The memory and persistence behaviors rest on Meta's own benchmarks, MemoryBench, FocusBench and SWE-Marathon, still awaiting independent confirmation. The model card admits its runs of rival models were best-effort and may not have been optimally tuned.

Why it matters

This is a pivot, not just a launch. Meta moves from an open-weights-only lab to a dual open-and-closed track, keeping its best variant inside a subscription. Artificial Analysis prices Wonder at $4.52 per evaluation task, against $1.60 for Spark 1.3 and $1.99 for Gemini 4 Argon, so stronger benchmarks do not automatically mean cheaper work. The question is institutional: persistent memory is powerful, and enterprises should demand retention and deletion controls before switching it on.
