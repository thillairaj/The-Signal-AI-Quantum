Decision-Making

Choosing a course of action under uncertainty, at the right speed, with the right level of input, and recording why. Good decision-makers match the process to the stakes and reversibility, decide, and then commit fully; poor ones either rush the irreversible or endlessly consult on the trivial. A decision made at 80 percent information and owned completely usually beats a perfect one made too late.

Key factJeff Bezos' distinction, from Amazon shareholder letters: Type 1 decisions are high-consequence and hard to reverse — make them slowly. Type 2 decisions are reversible — make them fast and let capable people make them.
Leader lensTake your oldest pending decision and classify it today: if it is reversible, decide within 24 hours with the information you already have and say so out loud.
