Target Run

The shooting gallery recall game from Play and Learn. Learn first on the cards, decks and tables, then come here to play it back from memory while the answers are moving.

How a run works

  • A stage is a short round of five questions for each player. Every question is scored on its own: nothing needs to run in a row.
  • A call at the top tells you what to find: the term that matches a meaning, or the full form of an abbreviation.
  • Questions are graded stage by stage from stage one onwards so the round stays fair and the challenge grows as you climb. Stages one to three are Simple, stages four to six are A little complex, stages seven to nine are More complex, and stage ten is Most complex. Nobody meets a harder question before its stage arrives.
  • The two formats taper as the stages rise. Stages one to three carry three meaning questions and two abbreviation questions in each round of five. Stages four and five carry four meaning questions and one abbreviation question. Stages six to ten carry meaning questions only, because abbreviations cannot grow more complex the way questions can.
  • Both players face the same shape of round at every stage. The five question slots follow one shared plan for the stage, and the plan is mirrored slot by slot: whatever format and difficulty the first player meets in a slot, the second player meets the same format and the same difficulty in that slot, with a different question. No question repeats anywhere in a match.
  • A small label above each call names the stage, the difficulty and the format of the question in play, so both players can see the round is balanced.
  • The wrong answers are built to resist guessing. Meaning questions sit beside near neighbours of the same kind and the same category, alike in length and shape, and the decoys grow closer in the later slots of a stage. Abbreviation questions use near miss expansions: each wrong bubble keeps the shape of the correct expansion with one word swapped for a close cousin from the same field, and every near miss is checked so it is never the true expansion of another abbreviation.
  • Every question opens with a fifteen second quick answer window: the call and all four answers stand still, fully on screen, and can be tapped from the first second. Solve inside the window and you earn the solve points plus a quick bonus of two points for every second left on the clock. A wrong burst inside the window still costs a heart, and the window keeps running. When the window ends, the targets start to move and the bonus is gone.
  • Answer targets glide across two lanes. Tap the right target to burst it. At most four answers for one question are in play at once, and the four answer bubbles in every call each carry a different bright colour, so the four choices are always easy to tell apart.
  • Targets glide faster at every stage and grow slightly smaller as you climb, and from stage five onward the targets hop as they move.
  • Burst the right target with no mistake on that question and it is a first attempt solve, worth 100 points. A wrong burst costs one heart and marks the question; burst the right target after that and it still solves, for 50 points.
  • If the right target glides off the edge, the question counts as missed and costs one heart. You hold three hearts each turn; lose the last one and your turn ends at once, with unplayed questions counted as missed.
  • Both players always play their full turn in a stage before any winner is worked out, so the comparison is equal.
  • The game pauses and waits before every turn: before the first turn of a match, and at every hand over in a two player game. A card names the player to play, and their turn begins only when they tap the Start button carrying their name. Nothing moves and no clock runs while the game waits.
  • The stage winner is decided by most first attempt solves, then most questions solved, then most points. Five first attempt solves is a perfect round; if both players are perfect, the stage is a tie.
  • After every stage the winner is announced, and you choose: continue to the next stage, or finish there. Finishing announces the match winner from stages won, with total points as the tiebreak. Completing stage ten completes the full ladder, and the final screen confirms that all ten stages were played.
  • A tap that hits nothing costs nothing but the moment. There is no deadline on answers once targets are moving: the pressure is target speed and your hearts.
