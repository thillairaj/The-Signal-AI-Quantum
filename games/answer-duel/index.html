Answer Duel

The card throwing recall game from Play and Learn. Learn first on the cards, decks and tables, then come here to play it back from memory.

How a match runs

  • Players answer one at a time, turn by turn. Nobody plays at the same moment as the other player.
  • Every question deals the player on turn four answer cards. Exactly one card is right.
  • Tap a card to throw it to the centre. A wrong card leaves your hand, and you choose again from the cards that stay.
  • Points fall with each choice: right on the first choice scores 100, the second choice scores 50, the third scores 25, and the fourth scores 0, because by then the right card is the only one left.
  • Every player always finishes the question, even at 0 points. One unknown question never knocks anyone out, so the contest stays alive and fair.
  • A match runs for five questions or ten questions; pick the length below. Turns alternate and Player One starts, so in a five question duel Player One answers three questions and Player Two answers two, while a ten question duel gives five questions each. In a one player match, one player answers every question. The higher total wins, and equal totals give a shared win.
  • Before every turn the game pauses on a ready card naming the player to play and how many questions that player answers in the match. Nothing starts until that player taps the Start button carrying their name.
  • The four cards of a question are always of one kind: four term names, four meanings or four full forms of like abbreviations, close in shape and length, so no card stands out as the odd one. The first one or two questions are the gentlest. From the third question on, the wrong cards come from the same corner of the subject as the right one and sit closer to it, and the closing questions use the closest cards in the pool. A guesser faces four believable cards; only a player who knows the answer can pick it with confidence.
  • There is no clock in this game. Only the order of your choices sets the score.
