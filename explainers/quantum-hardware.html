      Two machines built this way

      The fridge-and-chip design above is shared by the leading superconducting systems. Figures are as each company published them; last checked 28 October 2026.

      Willow

      Google Quantum AI

      Qubits
      105 transmons, square grid
      Two-qubit error
      0.33% on average
      Qubit lifetime (T1)
      68 µs (error-correction chip), 98 µs (benchmark chip)
      Error-correction cycles
      909,000 per second
      • Dec 2024 Showed errors falling as the error-correcting code grew from 3×3 to 7×7, the “below threshold” result.
      • Oct 2025 Announced “Quantum Echoes”, which Google describes as the first verifiable quantum advantage on hardware.
      • Oct 2026 Demonstrated magic-state cultivation, cutting errors about 40-fold compared with uncultivated states (published in Science).

      Nighthawk and Loon

      IBM Quantum

      Nighthawk qubits
      120, each linked to four neighbours
      Tunable couplers
      218
      Circuit size
      Up to 5,000 two-qubit gates
      Decoding speed
      Under 480 ns (real-time qLDPC)
      • Nov 2025 Nighthawk introduced as IBM’s chip for showing quantum advantage by the end of 2026.
      • Nov 2025 Loon, an experimental processor, demonstrated the building blocks IBM needs for fault tolerance.
      • Roadmap A large-scale, fault-tolerant system called Starling targeted for 2029; chip wafers now made in a 300 mm fab in Albany, New York.
