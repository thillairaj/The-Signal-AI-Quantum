Osaka links a home-built quantum computer to its supercomputer

The University of Osaka has built SEAFOOD, a hybrid platform linking its OCTOPUS supercomputer (2.293 PFLOPS) with domestically built superconducting quantum processors on separate campuses. The system is scheduled to enter formal operation in December 2026, and the university says it is the first time a Japanese-built quantum computer has been joined to an on-campus supercomputer at one academic institution. In a demonstration, the quantum processor sampled configurations for a four-hydrogen molecule while OCTOPUS handled the classical calculation, matching the classical reference result.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR TECH

Hybrid platforms show how near-term quantum hardware gets used alongside supercomputers, with each side doing the maths it handles best.
